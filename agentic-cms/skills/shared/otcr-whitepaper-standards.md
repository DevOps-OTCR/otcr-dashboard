# OTCR whitepaper standards

The standards a whitepaper review checks against. Each one has a status, and the status sets how a finding is worded. Where OTCR has not settled a point, section 7 gives the interim rule to apply.

## How much weight each standard carries

| Status | Meaning | How to word a finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Confirmed** | Stated in OTCR's White Paper + Research Training | "OTCR's whitepaper training says..." |
| **Observed** | Seen in the one example page in that training, but not stated as a rule | "OTCR's example whitepaper uses..." |
| **Default** | Generic good practice, not OTCR-specific | "by general convention..." |

## 1. Whitepaper make-up (Confirmed)

The training lists six parts, in this order:

1. Cover Page
2. Table of Contents
3. Research by Section
4. Insights
5. Overall Takeaways
6. Sources

Within the research, each section is a **Topic** (the overall section being researched), broken into **Subpoints** (where the research is set out), with **Citations** for the sources.

The training defines a whitepaper as a report or guide that informs readers concisely about a complex issue and presents the issuing body's view on it.

## 2. Citations (Confirmed)

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | Every meaningful piece of information in a deliverable should be cited. All non-obvious information must be backed by a source |
| 2 | In reports and whitepapers, citations are footnotes at the bottom of the page, made with Insert Footnote |
| 3 | Each footnote includes a link and a brief annotation saying what the source is used for |
| 4 | Citations should be easy for the reader to reach |

## 3. Source quality (Confirmed)

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 5 | Data shown to a client should be verified by an academic, industry, government or primary source |
| 6 | Strive to find the primary source of any piece of information |
| 7 | Wikipedia and similar sites are a starting point only: find and cite the original source |
| 8 | Blogs: find the primary source behind the post |
| 9 | Preferred sources: university business databases (company profiles, country profiles, market research), reports from major consulting firms and respected think tanks, academic search engines, and government sources such as the Census Bureau and the CIA World Factbook |
| 10 | Interviews, newspaper and magazine articles and other web pages should be used only when academic or industry research is not available |
| 11 | Search more than one database where possible; they make different assumptions and give different results |
| 12 | Subject-matter experts add credibility and catch errors in reasoning; seek them out where possible |

## 4. Research quality (Confirmed)

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 13 | All research should have an analysis component that shows why the research matters and makes it usable for the client |
| 14 | Follow the problem-solving approach: define the problem, structure it, prioritise the issues (the 80/20 rule), conduct the analysis, synthesise what is important for the client, and conclude with a recommendation |
| 15 | An issue tree is the recommended way to break a problem into parts, though not required every time |

## 5. Observed in the training's example page (not stated as rules)

- OTCR Consulting logo at the top right of the page
- Topic headings numbered ("2. Share Rates"); subpoint headings lettered ("a. Overview", "b. KPI Relevance")
- Headings bold, in title case; body in a sans-serif face, left-aligned
- Superscript footnote number placed after the sentence's punctuation
- Page number at the bottom right

The example's footnotes are bare links. Rule 3 still applies: an author following the example has not met the stated rule.

## 6. Not stated anywhere (generic defaults apply)

Fonts, sizes and margins; heading styles; captions for figures and tables; length; number, date and currency formats; grammar and punctuation conventions.

## 7. Unsettled points: interim rules

OTCR has not settled these. Word any finding that rests on one as a recommendation ("consider..."), never as a breach.

| Point | Interim rule |
|---|---|
| Layout and template | No OTCR whitepaper template exists to check against. Judge layout by general convention and section 5 only |
| Sources section | A Sources part is expected (section 1). If present, every footnoted source should appear in it; do not require a particular format |
| Insights placement | Accept one Insights section after the research or insights at the end of each topic. Report only research with no insight at all (rule 13) |
| Heading numbering | Do not require the "1." / "a." scheme; flag inconsistency within the paper only |
| Executive summary | Not part of the make-up. Recommend one where the answer is hard to find; never report its absence as a breach |
| Audience | Review as a client deliverable unless the request says it is internal |
| Source age | No fixed cutoff. Flag data that is old for a fast-moving topic, as a recommendation |
| Grammar and formatting | Apply generic conventions, worded as "by general convention..." |
