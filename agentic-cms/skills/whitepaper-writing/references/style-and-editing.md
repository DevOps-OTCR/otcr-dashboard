# Style and Editing

Editing is not proofreading. It is deciding what the reader must remember and removing everything that competes with it.

---

## 1. Three passes, in order

| Pass | Question | Do not |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Argument** | Does each section advance the thesis? | Fix sentences in a section that should be cut |
| **2. Structure** | Is the order right? Does the summary match? | Reorder before the argument is settled |
| **3. Sentence** | Is each sentence clear, specific, necessary? | Polish prose that will be cut |

Most drafting time is wasted on pass 3 before passes 1 and 2 are done.

---

## 2. Sentence diagnostics

| Symptom | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Nominalisation | "the implementation of a partnership strategy" | "partnering" / "we partner" |
| Stacked hedges | "it may potentially be somewhat beneficial" | "it is likely to help, if X" |
| Throat-clearing | "In order to begin to consider…" | Start at the verb |
| False agency | "the data suggests" (data suggests nothing) | "we read the data as" |
| Unsupported comparative | "a more mature ecosystem" | "more mature than <what>, on <measure>" |
| Abstract noun chain | "capability development enablement" | Name the capability and the action |
| Passive by habit | "a decision was reached" | "the board decided" |
| Em-dash pile-up | — three — in — one — sentence | One dash, or a full stop |
| Rule of three reflex | "faster, cheaper, and more scalable" | Keep only what the evidence supports |
| Trailing qualifier | "…, broadly speaking" | Delete |

**Average sentence length ~25 words. Paragraphs ≤5 sentences. One idea per paragraph.**

---

## 3. AI-prose tells to remove

| Tell | Why it reads as machine-written | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| "It is important to note that" | Adds nothing | The note itself |
| "In today's rapidly evolving landscape" | Empty frame | The specific change and date |
| "This presents both opportunities and challenges" | Unfalsifiable | Which opportunity, which challenge, for whom |
| "A holistic, robust approach" | Adjectives instead of a method | The method |
| "Leverage synergies" | Jargon masking an absent mechanism | "share X across Y", or delete |
| "Best-in-class" | Unmeasured comparison | A named benchmark |
| "Studies show" | Unattributed | The study, or remove |
| "It is clear that" | Earns nothing | State the evidence |
| Symmetrical "on one hand / on the other" | Balanced but undecided | Take the position, then state the condition |
| "In conclusion" as a section | Signals the previous pages did not conclude | Delete; conclusions arrive early |
| Every paragraph the same length | Rhythm of a template | Vary by function |

## 4. Before / after

**Weak:** "There are a number of significant challenges facing mid-market freight forwarders in relation to audit readiness, and it is important that these are addressed in a holistic manner."

**Better:** "Mid-market forwarders will face audit-readiness requirements within 18 months, and most lack the internal capacity to produce the evidence (S2, S5). This is a software problem with a services component, not a consulting problem."

**Why:** names the constraint, dates it, cites it, and takes a position on what kind of solution it is.

---

**Weak:** "The market is large and growing, representing a significant opportunity for the client."

**Better:** "Reachable spend is €3.9–4.2M at €22k ACV, assuming 12% channel reach. The reach assumption, not price, dominates the outcome (S4, S7)."

**Why:** a range, a unit, a named dominant assumption, and traceability.

---

**Weak:** "We recommend that the client considers developing a partnership strategy to enter the market."

**Better:** "We recommend entering through two audit firms rather than direct sales. The mechanism is that audit firms already own the buyer relationship and absorb implementation risk; our evidence of 6 comparable arrangements suggests a 15–20% attach rate. Kill criterion: fewer than 3 signed referrals by end of Q2."

**Why:** actor, action, mechanism, evidence, number, kill criterion.

## 5. Headings

A heading is a claim, not a label.

| Label | Claim |
|---|---|
| Market overview | Mid-market demand is concentrated in 3 countries |
| Competitive landscape | No incumbent serves mid-market audit evidence |
| Risks | Channel dependence is the single point of failure |
| Next steps | Two decisions are needed before Q1 |

If a heading could be moved to another document unchanged, it is too generic.

---

## 6. Numbers in prose

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Round to the precision the input supports | "≈€4M", not "€3.94M" |
| Always unit + period + basis | "€22k ACV, FY2026, list price" |
| Use one currency in the body | Convert in an appendix, state the rate |
| Percentages need a base | "35% of firms with 50–500 employees" |
| Ranges beat false precision | "€18–30k" |
| Compare like with like | Same definition, geography, period |

---

## 7. Final read-through checklist

- [ ] First sentence of every section carries its conclusion
- [ ] No paragraph could appear unchanged in another client's document
- [ ] Every "we recommend" has a mechanism and a condition
- [ ] Every number has a source or an `[ESTIMATE]` basis
- [ ] No assumption has been promoted in revision
- [ ] Headings read as an argument on their own
- [ ] Terminology is consistent (one name per concept)
- [ ] The summary would survive if the body were deleted
- [ ] Nothing is included to demonstrate effort
