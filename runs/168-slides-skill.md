# Harbor Lane Coffee — UIUC Wholesale Pilot
Governing thought: Harbor Lane should pilot wholesale to 4 of the 14 UIUC campus cafés at $11/lb, converting about a third of the Chicago-roaster accounts with existing spare capacity and no new equipment.

#1 Pilot wholesale to four campus cafés, funded entirely by Harbor Lane's idle roasting capacity
- 14 UIUC campus cafés; 3 buy from a Chicago roaster
- Those 3 each use ~40 lb/week, ~120 lb/week combined
- Pilot: 4 cafés [ESTIMATE: S1 + one adjacent non-roaster account as a control]
- 600 lb/week spare capacity comfortably absorbs ~160 lb/week
Source: S1 (café census, 14 cafés); S2 (40 lb/week/café); S6 (600 lb/week spare)

#2 Harbor Lane enters $1.50/lb below the incumbent, a 12% price advantage
- Harbor Lane $11.00/lb vs Chicago roaster $12.50/lb
- 12% gap [ESTIMATE: (12.50 − 11.00) ÷ 12.50]
- Competitor serves only 3 of 14 cafés; remaining 11 are unserved or self-supplied
- Margin headroom retained at pilot volume
Source: S3 ($11/lb); S4 ($12.50/lb)

#3 The three Chicago-roaster accounts are the natural first converts at the limit of the 600 lb/week headroom
- Each conversion adds ~40 lb/week of committed volume
- 3 conversions = 120 lb/week, ~20% of spare capacity
- Capacity ceiling implies a maximum of ~15 similar accounts before expansion
- Pricing advantage, not capacity, is the binding constraint at pilot scale
Source: S2, S6; derived ceiling [ESTIMATE: 600 ÷ 40]

#4 Conversion hinges on three tests: price, cup consistency, and delivery cadence
- Price: already a 12% advantage (S3, S4)
- Consistency: unproven — competitor has an established roast profile
- Cadence: UIUC cafés need twice-weekly drops; Harbor Lane has no wholesale route today
- Each café's buying decision is owned by a manager, not central procurement (I1)
Source: I1 (inferred from café census showing independent purchasing)

#5 Run a 12-week, 4-café pilot with explicit kill criteria
- Owner (proposed): Wholesale lead, Harbor Lane
- Phase 1 (weeks 1–4): onboard 2 cafés, weekly cupping check
- Phase 2 (weeks 5–12): add 2 more, track reorder rate
- Kill if reorder rate <70% by week 8 or cupping variance exceeds agreed tolerance
- Gate: LC sign-off before extending to remaining 10 cafés
Source: S1–S6; thresholds anchored to ledger values

#6 Pilot caps capital exposure at idle capacity while proving the wholesale motion
- Worst case: 4 cafés churn, ~0 net cost — capacity was idle
- Best case: 120–160 lb/week recurring, ~$7,000–9,400/month incremental revenue [ESTIMATE: 160 lb × $11 × 4.3 weeks]
- Decision requested: approve the 12-week pilot and name the owner
Source: S2, S3, S6
